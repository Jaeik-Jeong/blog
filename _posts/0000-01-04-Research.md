The following sections present my research interests.

# __Beyond Forecasting Accuracy: Deep Reinforcement Learning for Renewable Energy Management__<br/>

My research has focused on applying deep learning and reinforcement learning to renewable energy forecasting and energy management. The central idea behind my research is that renewable energy forecasting should consider not only the magnitude of prediction errors but also their compensability.<br/>
My first study investigated how forecasting errors from multiple photovoltaic sites could offset each other through aggregation, using a convolutional neural network (CNN) to capture spatial and temporal correlations.<br/>
Building on this idea, my second study introduced a new forecasting objective that considers the ability of energy storage systems (ESSs) to compensate for prediction errors. Using proximal policy optimization (PPO) with a long short-term memory (LSTM) network, I developed a deep reinforcement learning framework that minimizes the remaining errors after ESS compensation.<br/>
My third study further extended this approach by incorporating electricity market bidding strategies, jointly optimizing error compensation and economic profits. I also envisioned integrating the CNN from my first study with the LSTM-based reinforcement learning framework from my subsequent studies, although this direction remained unexplored after completing my Ph.D.<br/>
Beyond these core studies, I investigated the advantages of discrete reinforcement learning over continuous reinforcement learning for ESS energy arbitrage, broadening my perspective on reinforcement learning algorithms.<br/>
I also contributed to a collaborative study addressing integrated energy management under uncertainty, involving renewable energy, ESSs, and electricity markets.<br/>
Overall, my research has evolved from improving forecasting accuracy toward integrating forecasting, physical flexibility, and economic decision-making in renewable energy systems.<br/>

# __Robust Representation Learning for Imperfect Data: From Noise and Missing Values to Anomaly Detection__<br/>

My research on representation learning and unsupervised learning has focused on developing robust AI models that can effectively handle imperfect input data. The central idea is that real-world data often contain errors or missing values, and AI models should remain reliable under such conditions.<br/>
My first study explored representation learning using a Transformer encoder to improve photovoltaic power forecasting under weather forecast errors.<br/>
My second study investigated autoencoder-based feature extraction to achieve robust load forecasting even when input data contain missing values.<br/>
Building on these ideas, my third study extended the application of autoencoders beyond the energy domain to recommender systems, addressing both natural noise in observed ratings and missing ratings.<br/>
My fourth study further expanded the role of representation learning by combining convolutional autoencoders with clustering techniques for anomaly detection in photovoltaic systems. These studies demonstrated how learned representations can support various tasks involving noisy, incomplete, or abnormal data.<br/>
Finally, as the first author, I developed a denoising masked autoencoder framework for missing data imputation in electric load datasets, particularly under constrained data environments. Furthermore, the learned features exhibited approximately Gaussian distributions, suggesting potential robustness to outliers as well as missing values.<br/>
Overall, my research has evolved from learning robust representations under imperfect inputs toward detecting anomalies and reconstructing missing data, with the broader goal of improving the reliability and practical applicability of AI models in real-world environments.<br/>

# __Beyond AI for Energy Management: Toward Energy-Efficient AI Systems__<br/>

My previous research primarily focused on AI for Energy Management, applying deep reinforcement learning and representation learning to improve energy forecasting, system operation, and robustness under imperfect data. My recent research has expanded toward Energy Management for AI, recognizing that AI models themselves consume energy.<br/>
The central idea is that higher prediction accuracy does not necessarily lead to better overall energy efficiency when computational costs and hardware degradation are considered.<br/>
To explore this perspective, I investigated the trade-off between prediction accuracy and energy efficiency using models of varying complexity, including Transformers. I introduced a framework that jointly considers forecasting errors, inference energy consumption, and battery aging, demonstrating that more accurate models are not always the most energy-efficient.<br/>
Building on this foundation, my future research aims to develop energy-aware AI systems that balance predictive performance, computational efficiency, and physical constraints, ultimately bridging AI for Energy Management and Energy Management for AI.<br/>
